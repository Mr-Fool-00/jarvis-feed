### 2. Skill-MAS: Evolving Meta-Skills for Automatic Multi-Agent Systems — arXiv:2606.18837
**Source:** arXiv · arxiv.org/abs/2606.18837 · **Score:** 6/10

June 17, 2026 paper. Skill-MAS proposes a third path between "inference-time MAS" (agents that collaborate at runtime) and "training-time MAS" (fine-tuned agents): an **evolvable Meta-Skill layer** that accumulates experience across runs without touching model weights. The key decoupling: experience retention doesn't require parametric updates. Each agent maintains a meta-skill module that learns from past orchestration decisions and propagates successful patterns to future runs. The paper demonstrates that a fixed LLM backbone + evolving meta-skill matches or beats fine-tuned MAS on 4 benchmarks.

**Why it matters:** Leo's Council pattern (5-advisor + Chairman) is a fixed orchestration architecture — the prompts and roles don't change between runs. Skill-MAS's approach suggests the orchestration layer itself could accumulate "what worked" signals between fiction sessions (which chapter structures got approved, which quality checks caught real issues, which advisor roles consistently blocked progress). The evolvable meta-skill is closer to the `state/feedback.md` concept than to fine-tuning. Caveat: couldn't deep-read full paper (arXiv 403 in sandbox). Summary based on abstract + citation network. Worth a manual read.

🔗 https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18837

**Build verdict:** INFORMATIONAL — the concept of stateful orchestration that improves across runs is worth filing. Not immediately buildable with the current stack (requires training infrastructure), but the pattern — storing orchestration outcomes in a feedback file and biasing future runs — is partly implementable with state/feedback.md today.
