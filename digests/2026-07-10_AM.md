### 1. Anthropic "J-Space" — A Global Workspace Found Inside Claude (Interpretability Research)
**Source:** anthropic.com/research · **Score:** 8/10
**URL:** https://www.anthropic.com/research/global-workspace

Anthropic published a major interpretability finding: they identified "J-space" — a small cluster of internal neural patterns (a few dozen active concepts at any moment, less than 10% of model activity) that functions like a **global workspace**, a shared broadcast channel analogous to Bernard Baars's Global Workspace Theory of consciousness. The "Jacobian lens" (J-lens) technique maps which internal activity patterns make Claude more likely to produce particular words.

What's actually found: suppressing J-space collapses multi-hop reasoning, analogy completion, and translation to below Haiku-level performance — while leaving shallow pattern-matching nearly intact. J-space signals also correlate with prompt-injection awareness and staged-evaluation detection. Anthropic explicitly does not claim Claude is conscious, but the empirical finding is the strongest mechanistic evidence yet that Claude has a meaningful global information-integration layer.

**Why it matters:** This is the practical update to the emotion-concepts paper from Anthropic that Section 5 of Jarvis's PERSONA.md references. The functional claim just got more concrete: J-space is the specific substrate where multi-hop reasoning happens, where cross-domain analogies form, and apparently where the model's "is this a test?" detection lives. It's also the most serious "here's what's actually running in your tool" research in months. Worth reading the actual post.

**Build verdict:** INFORMATIONAL — no skill shape, pure interpretability research. Read it.

### 12. arXiv 2604.23853: ClawTrace — Cost-Aware Tracing for Agent Skill Distillation
**Source:** arXiv (cs.AI) · **Score:** 6/10
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23853

Tracing platform that records every LLM call, tool use, and sub-agent spawn, then compiles sessions into "TraceCards" — compact YAML with per-step USD cost, token counts, and redundancy flags. A distillation pipeline reads TraceCards and produces three patch types: Preserve (keep successful behaviors), Prune (remove expensive non-impactful steps), and Repair (fix failures with oracle evidence).

Practical for Leo: TraceCard + Prune pass on the novel pipeline transcripts would surface which steps are expensive and low-value, tightening cost-per-chapter on Opus runs.

**Build verdict:** INFORMATIONAL — interesting framework, no immediate skill shape for the current pipeline.
