### 1. arxiv:2607.05577 — Narrative World Model: Narratology-Grounded Writer Memory for Long-Form Fiction
**Source:** arxiv.org (PocketFM) · **Score:** 8/10 · **Build-worthy: YES**

From PocketFM (serialized audio fiction platform), this paper proposes a writer-memory system that directly addresses cross-chapter consistency in long-form fiction. Two interlocking mechanisms: (1) a **typed temporal-state graph** where every fact/relationship/epistemic boundary carries chapter-indexed validity intervals (bitemporal — you can query "what did character A believe in chapter 15" without contamination from chapter 22 revelations), and (2) **query-conditioned hybrid retrieval** (BM25 + vector + one-hop graph expansion) that assembles **chapter-safe evidence packets** scoped to chapters 1–N when writing chapter N.

The extraction schema defines six typed record families: *focalization/epistemic state* (who knows what and when they learned it), *event-vs-reveal order* (story-time vs. discourse-time — critical for flashbacks and unreliable narrators), *dramatic function* (scene beat labels), *promise/payoff tracking* (open/closed status on Chekhov's guns), and *relationship deltas* (directional change timestamped to chapter). All records anchor to the temporal graph with validity intervals.

Benchmark: 576 multi-hop narratological QA questions across 12 Gutenberg books + 5 production serialized books, evaluated with a held-constant Claude Opus reader. NWM scores **0.709** on the 110-question multi-hop subset vs. **0.582** for Graphiti/Zep (p = 0.054 McNemar paired test). The ablation confirms the gain is structural — it holds even when the Graphiti baseline uses NWM's own extractor. Graphiti/Zep, GraphRAG, and flat RAG all fail on the four question classes NWM targets: epistemic boundaries, reveal-order, promise/payoff, and relationship arcs.

Code stated as available "at the respective repositories" — no confirmed public GitHub URL yet (preprint under review, submitted July 6, 2026). Check the PDF directly or watch the PocketFM GitHub org.

🔗 https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05577

**Why it matters:** This is the exact memory architecture pattern your fiction pipeline is missing. You have Scribe/Editor analogs but no typed temporal graph, no epistemic-boundary records, no promise/payoff tracking, and no chapter-safety gate on retrieval. The paper's six record types and chapter-safe packet discipline are directly implementable as a Claude Sonnet extraction step after each finalized chapter. The benchmark also gives you a ready-made eval harness on Gutenberg books. Briefing filed.

### 2. Claude Mythos discovers attack on HAWK post-quantum cryptography
**Source:** anthropic.com · **Score:** 7/10 · Build-worthy: NO (informational)

On July 28, Anthropic published that Claude Mythos Preview — running in a multi-agent scaffold on Claude Code, with access to math software and research databases — discovered a **previously unknown "nontrivial automorphism"** in HAWK-256's lattice structure, reducing its key-recovery cost from ~2^64 to ~2^38 operations (67 million times less work, key strength effectively halved). HAWK had survived two rounds of human expert review over two years without a successful attack. Claude Mythos found it in approximately **60 hours of autonomous work at ~$100,000 API cost.**

HAWK is the only lattice-based scheme among nine candidates NIST advanced to the third round of its additional post-quantum digital-signature process in May 2026. The resulting academic paper "HAWK: Having Automorphisms Weakens Key" was published simultaneously at IACR. Authors of that paper state: *"The majority of mathematical discoveries in this paper were AI-assisted. Human author contribution mainly consisted of directing, organizing and verifying AI work."*

Secondary finding: Claude named a "Möbius Bridge" technique that eliminates a 2^56 guessing step in a meet-in-the-middle attack against 7-round AES-128, making it 200–800× faster. Purely theoretical (requires 2^105 chosen plaintexts; production AES-128 is 10 rounds). Additional findings: key recovery against 13-round LEA (under 1 hour on desktop), 6-round Serpent-128, plus improvements on Salsa20, Poseidon, SHA-1. Anthropic also released **CryptanalysisBench** (191 tasks, with ETH Zurich/Tel Aviv/Haifa/TU Berlin) alongside the disclosure.

HAWK has not been deployed; NIST has not yet publicly announced whether it will revise parameters or reconsider the scheme's standing.

🔗 https://www.anthropic.com/research/discovering-cryptographic-weaknesses
🔗 https://cic.iacr.org/p/2/2/20 (IACR paper)

**Why it matters:** Not directly actionable for your fiction/CC stack, but this is a historically significant result — the first time a frontier AI has materially advanced the cryptanalysis field against a live NIST standardization candidate.
