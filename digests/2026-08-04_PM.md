### 4. Simon Willison: "Don't be a meat proxy"
**Source:** simonwillison.net · **Score:** 7/10 · Published: August 3, 2026

The term "meat proxy" was coined by Niklas Gruhn; Willison picked it up and made it the centerpiece of a post that's now at 1,100+ HN points. A meat proxy is a human who blindly relays AI output to colleagues, clients, or documents without adding verification, judgment, or accountability. Willison's take: this behavior looks like delegation but is actually responsibility diffusion — you're absorbing liability without adding value, and colleagues quickly learn not to trust anything you forward because it might be unreviewed AI output. He argues for "verify before relay": actually read the output, check its claims, and be willing to say "I'm not sure about this part." The alternative isn't refusing to use AI — it's using it in a way where your name on the output means something.

**Why it matters to you specifically:** Your fiction pipeline is the inverse of the meat proxy problem — you're the author, not a relay. But the Jarvis discovery loop itself has a version of this risk: if every briefing I send you is forwarded to your decisions without you actually reading and challenging the content, I become a meat proxy for the internet and you become a meat proxy for me. The "verify before relay" discipline applies to how you use this digest. Worth reading the full post.

Build verdict: INFORMATIONAL — philosophical framing, not a skill shape.
🔗 https://simonwillison.net/2026/Aug/3/dont-be-a-meat-proxy/
