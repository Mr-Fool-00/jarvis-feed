## The concept

**Meat proxy** (coined by Niklas Gruhn; popularized by Willison's post):

A meat proxy is a human who blindly relays AI output to colleagues, clients, or documents without adding verification, judgment, or accountability. It looks like delegation but functions as responsibility diffusion: you absorb the liability of authorship without contributing the judgment that authorship normally implies.

### Why it's a failure mode

1. **Trust degrades asymmetrically.** A colleague who receives several unreviewed AI outputs from you learns quickly that your name on something doesn't mean you stand behind it. Recovering that trust is harder than the original credibility would have been worth.

2. **The AI's error rate is not zero.** Relaying unreviewed AI output means your error rate is now the model's error rate, not yours. In domains where you have expertise, the AI's mistakes are often ones you'd catch immediately — catching them is your value-add.

3. **You absorb liability without understanding.** When the output causes a problem (wrong advice, misattributed claim, hallucinated fact), you can't defend or correct it because you didn't read it carefully enough to know what it said.

---

## Willison's "verify before relay" framing

The post argues for "verify before relay": actually read the output, check its claims against what you know, and be willing to say explicitly "I'm not sure about this part." This is not a refusal to use AI — it's using AI in a way where your name on the output means something.

The piece also distinguishes this from the opposite failure: refusing to use AI out of pride or habit. Both are suboptimal. The meat proxy offloads too much; the AI-refuser doesn't offload enough. The productive middle is human-reviewed AI assistance.
