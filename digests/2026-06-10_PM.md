### 3. Anthropic Engineering: "How We Built Our Multi-Agent Research System"
**Source:** anthropic.com/engineering · **Score:** 8/10  
Anthropic published a detailed engineering post on the multi-agent architecture behind Claude's Research feature. The system uses an **orchestrator-worker pattern**: a Lead Researcher agent plans a research strategy, then fans out to parallel subagent workers who each handle a search scope. The lead then synthesizes. Results vs. single-agent setup: **90% improvement on complex queries, 15× token consumption**. Concrete engineering lessons from building it:

- **Search strategy mirrors expert human research.** Start with short, broad queries. Evaluate what's available. Progressively narrow. Don't start specific — you miss the landscape.
- **Early failure modes were severe.** First versions spawned 50 subagents for simple queries, scoured endlessly for nonexistent sources, and the workers "distracted each other with excessive updates" in shared context. All required explicit prompt engineering guardrails to fix.
- **Lead agent model choice matters more than worker model choice.** Claude Opus 4 as lead + Claude Sonnet 4 as workers outperformed a single Opus 4 by 90%. Skill gap between coordinator and executor is intentional.

**Why it matters:** This is the official blueprint for how Anthropic builds multi-agent systems internally — and the architecture maps directly to your book pipeline. Your writing pipeline is already a coordinator (the outline/planner) + workers (writers, fixers, reviewers). The specific failure modes they hit — subagents spawning too many sub-subagents, searching for things that don't exist, coordinator losing signal in worker noise — are your failure modes too. The "start broad, narrow progressively" search pattern applies to chapter research phases. The 15× token cost stat is a useful calibration: if you run a multi-agent pass on a chapter, expect 15× the cost of a single-agent pass. Plan your Max budget accordingly.

**Build verdict:** INFORMATIONAL — architectural inspiration. The "expert research pattern" (broad→narrow→synthesize) is directly applicable to your world-bible research phase. No skill to build; apply the pattern.

🔗 https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system
