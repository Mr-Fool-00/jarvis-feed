### 2. From Personas to Plot: Multi-Agent World-State Story Generation (arXiv:2607.00918)
**Source:** arXiv · Princeton / Pocket FM / UMich / UMD / UPF · **Score:** 8/10
**Build verdict:** BUILD-WORTHY — architecture blueprint for novel-length generation with 50% hallucination reduction. Briefing committed to `briefings/2026-07-03_magnet-atlas-fiction-pipeline.md`.

Submitted July 1, 2026. Addresses the core failure mode of LLM long-form fiction: **narrative drift and character contradiction at scale**. Tested up to 100 pages of output.

**Two-component system:**

**MAGNET** (Multi-Agent Goal-driven NarraTive Engine):
- Each character is a separate agent with a fixed persona
- Agents propose actions against a **shared world state** — a structured representation of story history
- Characters also track evolving story goals, so proposals are constrained by *who they are* and *what the story is trying to do*
- Genuine multi-agent: agents interact through the world state, not a single monolithic context

**ATLAS** (the evaluation layer):
- Builds scene-level world-state graphs across the generated story
- Compares graphs scene-to-scene to catch inconsistencies (character in two places, dead character speaking, timeline violations)
- Runs post-hoc over MAGNET's output — a verifier, not a generator
- **Separable:** ATLAS can be applied to any long-form generation pipeline as a standalone consistency checker

**Benchmark results at 100 pages:**
- vs. single-model baseline: **50% fewer hallucinations, 41% fewer annotation inconsistencies**
- vs. IBSEN (prior multi-agent story baseline): **45% fewer hallucinations, 34% fewer annotation inconsistencies**

No novel model training required. Existing LLMs as agent backbone.

**Direct application for Leo's pipeline:** MAGNET maps cleanly to chapter-chapter generation with persistent character agents. ATLAS is independently deployable as a nightly consistency audit over any novel draft.

🔗 https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00918

### 4. Narrative Flattening: Post-Training Compresses Thematic, Affective, and Stylistic Variation (arXiv:2605.27878)
**Source:** arXiv · Knowledge Lab, University of Chicago · **Score:** 7/10
**Build verdict:** BUILD-WORTHY — this is the diagnostic framework for why AI fiction feels flat. Directly changes model selection rationale for Leo's pipeline. Briefing committed to `briefings/2026-07-03_narrative-flattening.md`.

Published May 27, 2026. Empirically documents **narrative flattening**: post-training causes LLMs to produce fiction that is fluent but dynamically inert — stories with less thematic variation, emotional intensity, and stylistic diversity than both human writing and the base model.

Tested across three corpora (StoryStar, TMAS, The New Yorker) at four OLMo 32B checkpoints (Base → SFT → DPO → RLVR). Every post-training stage compresses creative range. The RL stage (RLVR) can recover roughly 5% of lost diversity — but only if the model hasn't already fully collapsed earlier.

**Practitioner takeaway:** Any pipeline using a standard instruction-tuned model is operating under narrative flattening by default. Base or lightly SFT'd models are the most stylistically diverse starting point — at the cost of controllability.

🔗 https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27878

### 5. Self-Compacting Language Model Agents — Model-Driven Context Management (arXiv:2606.23525)
**Source:** arXiv · ASAP AI · HuggingFace Papers · **Score:** 7/10
**Build verdict:** BUILD-WORTHY — maps directly to fiction pipeline stage boundaries. 18.1-point accuracy improvement, 30–67% token cost reduction. Briefing committed to `briefings/2026-07-03_self-compacting-agents.md`.

SelfCompact lets the model itself decide when to compact accumulated context — treating compaction as a callable tool rather than a fixed-interval external trigger.

Two components:
- **A compaction tool** the model can invoke at any point (extra LLM call, KV-cache reuse makes it affordable)
- **A natural-language rubric** specifying when to fire (sub-task resolved; trajectory converging) and when to suppress (mid-derivation; stuck, may need to backtrack)

The rubric is load-bearing — tool without rubric yields inconsistent behavior.

**Results:** +18.1 accuracy points on competition math; +5–9 points on agentic search (BrowseComp-Plus); 30–67% per-question token cost reduction.

**Maps to fiction:** Stage boundaries — end of scene, end of chapter, end of act — are natural compaction points. Mid-scene (the model is mid-draft, may need to backtrack) is a natural suppression point. The rubric writes itself from fiction pipeline stage semantics.

🔗 https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23525
