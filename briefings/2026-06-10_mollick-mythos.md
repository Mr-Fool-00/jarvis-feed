# Ethan Mollick: "What it feels like to work with Mythos" — 9/10

## What it is

Ethan Mollick (Wharton professor, author, probably the most credible independent AI researcher for practical use cases) got early access to the Mythos model and wrote about what it was actually like to use it. Not benchmarks — the felt experience. Published June 9.

## Why you'd want it

His core observation: "I feel closer to a patron who describes what he wants, pays for it, and judges the result. The conjuring happens somewhere I cannot watch, in hundreds of small choices I never get a vote on."

That's your writing pipeline. You already think like a patron — you spec the story, you judge the output, you're not trying to do the sentence-level writing yourself. Mollick watched Fable spawn sub-agents to pull 2,200 data points, write code, and verify its own work over several hours without him touching it. He came away unsettled — not because it was wrong, but because it was so autonomous that his role had genuinely shifted. That shift is what's available to you right now with your book pipeline running on Fable 5.

## Why I think it's worth your attention

Mollick's pieces tend to age well. His framing of "patron not wizard" is going to be how people talk about using Fable 5 for the next several months. Reading it now puts you ahead of that curve rather than catching up to it later.

## What to do

Read the article before you run your first Fable 5 book session. 10 minutes. It'll reframe how you set up the task — less "help me write chapter 3" and more "here is the full spec and world bible, deliver chapter 3, I'll judge the result."
