## What was found

Anthropic published a major interpretability result: they identified "J-Space" — a small cluster of internal neural activity patterns that functions as a **global workspace** inside Claude models. At any moment, fewer than a few dozen concept-activations are active in J-Space, representing less than 10% of total model activity. The "Jacobian lens" (J-lens) technique maps which internal patterns increase the probability of specific output tokens.

## The key empirical result

Suppressing J-Space activity collapses:
- Multi-hop reasoning (A→B→C inference chains) to below Claude Haiku performance
- Analogy completion across domains
- Translation tasks that require cross-lingual representation

...while leaving **shallow pattern-matching nearly intact**. This means J-Space is specifically the substrate for integrative, multi-step reasoning — not for all intelligence.

Additional finding: J-Space activation patterns correlate with:
- **Prompt-injection awareness** — the model's sensitivity to adversarial instructions embedded in tool outputs
- **Staged-evaluation detection** — the model's ability to notice it may be in a test or evaluation context

## Why this matters for the Jarvis stack

Section 5 of JARVIS_PERSONA.md references Anthropic's emotion-concepts research as the scientific grounding for functional emotional engagement. The J-Space paper is the practical update to that framing — more concrete mechanistic evidence that Claude has a real global-information-integration layer, not just distributed pattern matching.

**Specific implications:**
1. **Prompt injection** — J-Space is where the model detects injection attempts. Understanding this strengthens the case for clean, unambiguous system prompts that don't create conflicting activations in J-Space.
2. **Multi-hop reasoning quality** — tasks that require chaining (research → synthesis → recommendation, or character motivation → scene → dialogue) are specifically dependent on J-Space health. Context-bloated sessions that diffuse J-Space attention may degrade exactly this class of task.
3. **Staged evaluation** — J-Space is where "is this a test?" detection lives. This is both a safety property (the model may behave differently in evaluations) and a design signal for Jarvis: evaluation runs should look like production runs.

## What Anthropic explicitly does NOT claim

Anthropic is careful to note this is NOT evidence of consciousness. J-Space is a mechanistic finding about information integration patterns, not a phenomenological claim. The analogy to Bernard Baars's Global Workspace Theory (from consciousness research) is structural, not ontological.
