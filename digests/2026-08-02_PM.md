OpenAI published a math blog post. Gizmodo's headline nailed it: *"OpenAI Smuggled the Announcement of Astra Into a Blog Post About Math."*

What Astra actually did: solved **ten previously open mathematical problems**, each formalized in Lean 4 (machine-checkable, line by line). Total compute cost: **~$2,000 for all ten combined** — expressed in Sol API equivalent rates. Astra uses a multi-agent architecture: a root agent that spawns subagents, distributes work, waits on partial results, and synthesizes. Sessions ran over hours or days per problem.

The ten problems are in the `openai/ten-proofs` GitHub repo (Apache-2.0, Lean 4.32.0 + mathlib). The headline result: **non-sofic groups** — the first explicit construction of a non-sofic group ever, resolving a central group theory question open since Gromov introduced soficity in 1999. Other notable results: Connes's Rigidity Conjecture (disproved), Erdős Ramsey problem #183 (superexponential lower bounds), sphere packing bounds reaching the Cohn-Elkies threshold. Each result includes a Lean proof, a chain-of-thought walkthrough, and an LLM-generated PDF reconstructing how the proof was found.

**Why it matters:** This is the second lab-scale AI research demonstration in 72 hours — Claude's crypto findings on July 28 used the same human-as-project-manager architecture. The pattern is now clearly replicable: you set the goal, the agentic AI does the sustained reasoning. For your fiction pipeline: the same architecture that solved Gromov's group theory question also works for maintaining a coherent 20-chapter arc — the limiting factor is goal clarity and recovery from dead ends (see Aug 1 PM items 1 and 2), not raw capability. The $2,000 total cost for 10 hard math problems also suggests the economic floor for this kind of sustained agentic work is lower than most people think.

**Build verdict: INFORMATIONAL** — landmark; no standalone skill. Absorb the project-manager architecture. If you want to reproduce the multi-agent root-subagent pattern, the openai/ten-proofs repo has the Lean scaffolding worth studying structurally.
